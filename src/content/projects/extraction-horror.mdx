My internship at Pixoul Gaming, a VR and esports venue in Abu Dhabi. The brief was to design an
original VR-oriented game and get its central experience playable in eight weeks, built so that VR and the venue's
physical feedback hardware could be plugged in later.

You're a contractor sent into an abandoned nuclear facility. Find the fuel rods, use a Geiger counter and a three-second
scan to tell the three live ones from the decoys, carry them back one at a time, survive a twenty-second extraction and get out.
All of that while the Owl hunts you. It's an entity you can't kill.

## The Owl

The Owl patrols, investigates, pursues, searches and disengages. The interesting part is what it's allowed to know:

- Confirmed sight outranks sound.
- A sound tells it where something happened, not where you are now.
- Last-seen and last-heard positions are tracked separately, so hiding after making a noise actually works.
- It adapts to the routes you keep using, but deterministically and within limits, and it resets every run. No machine learning, no hidden profile of you across runs.

## Readable stealth

The goal for v0.3 was stealth you can reason about without a stealth meter. Visibility comes from three samples on the
player body (concealed, noticeable, exposed). Detection builds up over time instead of triggering instantly. Crouching,
walking, sprinting, landing and impacts all make different amounts of noise. Thrown metal objects and machinery panels
give you distractions. Full cover followed by a quiet relocation is always a valid way out.

## The facility

v0.4 rebuilt the level around readability: an asymmetric three-lobed facility with distinct route choices, landmarks and
pursuit loops. I drew the floor plan and then built it by hand from modular wall pieces.

For the look, I wrote a PSX-style shader and post-processing stack on Unity 6's Render Graph and lit the facility in URP.

## Engineering

Player, objectives, threat logic, feedback and hardware adapters all sit behind narrow interfaces, so the desktop
controls can be swapped for XR without touching the core game. Runs are seeded, so a bug or a playtest can be replayed
exactly. The project shipped with 148 Edit Mode and Play Mode tests and Windows builds.
